MOST IMPORTANT CORRECTIONS
- Q5: ESX uses maximum of THREE activity estimates, not simply maximum of two averages. See §5.3; the fast and slow EMA plus current-sample adjusted value are explicitly discussed.
- Q9/Q55: 1995 dissertation proves correct lottery compensation does NOT permit a client to steal expected CPU by yielding early. It increases win frequency to offset shorter consumption.
- Q12: Dec 2010 patch avoids wasted retries on PPN_NOTNEEDED before the old 16-refusal cap; the author described it as a SMALL OPTIMIZATION, not an unbounded-loop memory safety fix.
- Q13: the Nutanix patent documents buffering swap-bound pages and attempting alternate reclaim, not a proof of a particular CVM deadlock or universal deployment.
- Q17: CCGrid 2024 implements sharing-aware migration and measures destination expansion under conventional schemes. The source memory-saving relationship alone is not part of migrated guest bytes.
- Q19: QEMU CPR stops guest execution; cpr-exec preserves shared RAM and selected state across QEMU exec but is not a guarantee of zero pause or arbitrary host kernel replacement.
- Q49: SHARDS' reported median MAE is a measurement over 124 traces, not a deterministic guarantee for an adversarial trace.
- Q52: 101-client standard stride can generate 50-quanta transient error despite its 1-quantum PAIRWISE guarantee.
- Q58: CFS/EEVDF and stride share virtual-time ideas but are not the same implementation or a proven direct derivation of modern Linux code.

- A patent describes a claimed design. It does not prove product installation, workload performance or an actual interview topic.
